# Lab Proof: First Match ≠ Right Match
### Relevance Scoring & Rerankers — EU AI Act + Trustworthy AI Podcast

---

## System Overview

- **Sources:** EU AI Act (144-page PDF, extracted with `pdfplumber`), Trustworthy AI podcast (15.6 min audio, transcribed via OpenAI Whisper API)
- **Chunking:** 500 tokens/chunk, 75-token overlap, metadata tagged with `source` and `section`
- **Index:** Pinecone, 354 vectors, `text-embedding-3-small` (1536 dims), cosine similarity
- **Rerankers tested:** LLM relevance scoring (GPT-4o-mini, similarity/LLM blend) and Cohere `rerank-english-v3.0`
- **Pipeline:** `baseline_search` → `cohere_rerank` → context assembly → `gpt-4o-mini` answer generation
- **Validation:** all retrieval/reranking objects typed with pydantic (`RetrievedChunk`, `RerankedChunk`) to catch malformed metadata at the retrieval boundary

- EU_AI_Act / page_33_chunk_2 (rerank_score=0.9997) — retrieved, not cited

- EU_AI_Act / page_15_chunk_1 (rerank_score=0.9983) — retrieved, not cited

## Query 2: "What record-keeping obligations apply to high-risk AI systems?"

### Retrieved documents (baseline, top 5)
`page_59_chunk_0`, `page_33_chunk_1`, `page_20_chunk_2`, `page_61_chunk_1`, `page_19_chunk_1`

### Reranking process
This query is the clearest positive case for reranking. Cohere reranking pulled in `page_62_chunk_0` (Article 16 — providers' obligation to *retain* the logs) — a chunk that **did not appear in the baseline top-5 at all**. Without reranking, the answer only discusses Article 12 (systems must be capable of logging). With reranking, the answer adds Article 16 (providers must actually keep those logs) — a distinct, complementary legal obligation that pure similarity search missed entirely.

### Final answer (with reranking)
> High-risk AI systems have specific record-keeping obligations as outlined in the EU AI Act. According to Article 12, these systems must technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over their lifetime... Additionally, Article 16 specifies that providers of high-risk AI systems must keep the logs automatically generated by their systems (Article 16(e)). These obligations ensure traceability and compliance with the relevant requirements throughout the lifetime of the AI system.
>
> Sources: [EU_AI_Act / page_59_chunk_0] (Article 12), [EU_AI_Act / page_62_chunk_0] (Article 16)

### Evidence supporting the answer
Without reranking: 5 sources retrieved, answer cites only Article 12 content.
With reranking: 5 sources retrieved (`page_59_chunk_0`, `page_62_chunk_0`, `page_20_chunk_2`, `page_33_chunk_2`, `page_15_chunk_1`), answer explicitly cites **2** distinct articles by section — the best grounding-to-citation ratio of the three test queries.

## Limitation / Failure Case

**Retrieved ≠ cited: the pipeline cannot prove what actually grounded an answer, only what was fed to the LLM.**

Across all three test queries, the pipeline retrieves and reranks 5 chunks per query, but the LLM's generated answer explicitly cites only 1–2 of them (measured with a regex-based grounding checker comparing retrieved `section` IDs against sections named in the answer text). For Query 1, this meant **80% of retrieved evidence was unused and unaccounted for** — the printed "evidence used" list is technically accurate (those chunks were retrieved and included in the prompt context) but misleading as a grounding claim, since most of that content may have been ignored by the model, or blended into the answer without attribution.

**Risk:** A reviewer trusting the evidence list as proof of grounding would overestimate how much of the retrieved context actually supports the stated answer. This is a genuine trustworthiness gap for a system whose stated goal is traceability.

**Mitigation not yet implemented:** constrain the generation prompt to require an explicit citation-to-chunk mapping for every claim (e.g., structured output with `claim` + `supporting_chunk_id` pairs), then validate post-hoc that every citation in the answer maps to a retrieved chunk ID and flag any retrieved chunk that went uncited so it can be dropped from the evidence list shown to the user.

**Secondary limitation:** Cohere's rerank scores clustered near 1.0 across all candidates in Query 1 (0.997–1.000), providing weak discrimination once the baseline candidate set is already strong. It behaves more like a relevant/irrelevant filter than a fine-grained ranker in this setting.

## Data Quality Note

Initial PDF extraction with `pypdf` produced broken text spacing (e.g. `"Ar ticle 12"`, `"T o address"`) that would have degraded LLM relevance judgments. Switched to `pdfplumber`, which produced clean extraction. Also caught and fixed a Pinecone index consistency bug where re-running `data_prep.py` after the extractor swap left 107 stale/dirty vectors in the index (461 total vs. 354 newly upserted) because vector IDs were assigned by list position; resolved by clearing the index before each full re-index.
